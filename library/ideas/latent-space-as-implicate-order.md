# Idea — Latent space as implicate order

**Added:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** `[source]` — metaphysics. Filed as motivation, not mechanism.

## Statement

A model's latent space may reflect a fundamental enfolded mathematical structure of
information — echoing David Bohm's *implicate order*, in which the explicit world unfolds
from an enfolded whole where everything is contained in everything.

## Why this note exists

**This is not testable and should not be treated as if it were.** It is filed for two
reasons, both practical.

First, honesty about the source: the philosophy and the engineering claims arrive in the
same register in the interview, and separating them is our job. Doing that separation in
writing, once, is cheaper than re-litigating it every time the vocabulary reappears.

Second, and less dismissively: the analogy is not *empty*, it is *unearned*. There is a
real structural resemblance — distributed representations genuinely are holographic in a
loose sense; information about a concept is spread across dimensions rather than localised,
and any sufficiently large subspace carries a degraded version of the whole. That
resemblance is a property of distributed encoding, which is well understood and needs no
metaphysics. The unearned step is treating the resemblance as evidence of a shared
underlying reality.

The failure mode to guard against is an analogy quietly becoming a mechanism: "latent space
is like the implicate order" → "latent space *is* an implicate order" → "therefore
properties of the implicate order transfer to models." The third step imports conclusions
the analogy never licensed.

## What can be salvaged

The nearest defensible technical statements, which stand on their own without Bohm:

- Distributed representations spread information across dimensions rather than localising it
  — superposition. `[lit]` (Elhage et al., "Toy Models of Superposition", 2022)
- Representations exhibit structure with far lower intrinsic dimension than their ambient
  dimension. `[lit]`
- Hierarchical concepts appear in geometrically regular arrangements. `[lit]`

These are measurable and are what [[library/ideas/reasoning-as-internal-geometry]] should be
built on. Bohm adds nothing to them operationally.

## Honest assessment

Bohm's implicate order is contested *within physics* — a minority interpretation, viewed by
critics as poetic metaphysics resting on analogy, and by defenders as a serious attempt at a
cosmology integrating physics and mind. Importing a contested framework from one field to
motivate claims in another does not add support to either. It adds vocabulary.

That said: motivation is allowed to be unfalsifiable. Plenty of good physics has been
motivated by aesthetics. The rule here is only that motivation never appears in a results
section, and that no experiment's design depends on it being true.
