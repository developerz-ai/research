# Synthesis — Freshly-generated paired perturbations are the only cheap defence against contamination

**Added:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** `[open]` — the argument is sound; the magnitude is unmeasured

## Ingredients

| Idea | From | Contributes |
|---|---|---|
| [[library/ideas/flip-rate-evaluation]] | Sophontic + contrast sets | paired items where one load-bearing fact changes |
| [[library/ideas/data-quality-over-scale]] | phi / TinyStories line of work | small models trained on synthetic data derived from larger models |
| [[library/ideas/frozen-giants]] | Michels | models are static, so their training cutoff is a fixed, knowable boundary |

## The combination

**New:** the small-model literature has a structural contamination problem that the
large-model literature does not, and paired perturbation evaluation is the only defence
cheap enough for a small lab to actually deploy.

The argument:

- **Inherited:** the strongest small-model results come from training on synthetic data
  generated by frontier models ("textbook quality"). This is the core of the phi line of work.
- **Inherited:** benchmark contamination is a known, widespread problem.
- **New:** these compound in a way that is specific to this setting. When a small model is
  trained on data generated by a large model that has itself seen the benchmark, benchmark
  content can reach the small model **without appearing anywhere in its training corpus** —
  laundered through the generator into paraphrases that no n-gram overlap check will catch.
  Standard decontamination assumes the leak is textual. Here it is semantic, and the usual
  detection method is structurally blind to it.
- **Therefore:** every "small model beats large model on benchmark X" claim — including the
  ones we cite approvingly in [[library/ideas/data-quality-over-scale]] — has a plausible
  alternative explanation that its authors could not have ruled out with the tools they used.

Paired perturbations dodge the whole problem: generate the pairs yourself, never publish
them, edit the load-bearing fact so the memorised answer becomes the *wrong* one. A
contaminated model does not merely fail to benefit — it is actively penalised, because
recall of the original answer produces an error on the perturbed member.

That asymmetry is the useful part. Most decontamination methods try to prove absence of
leakage; this one makes leakage *cost* the model something.

## Why nobody has said this

Contamination work focuses on detection (n-gram overlap, membership inference) — proving a
negative, expensively. The contrast-set literature predates the synthetic-data era and was
motivated by spurious correlations, not leakage. Nobody appears to have connected
"synthetic training data from a contaminated teacher" to "contrast sets happen to be immune
to exactly this."

## What it would predict

- Models trained heavily on frontier-generated synthetic data show a **larger gap** between
  canonical accuracy and flip rate than models trained on natural corpora at matched size.
- That gap should be **largest on well-known public benchmarks** and smallest on freshly
  generated items — the signature of recall rather than reasoning.
- **Not implied by contamination work alone:** it predicts inflated scores, but has no
  account of a differential effect on paired items.
- **Not implied by contrast-set work alone:** it predicts shortcut sensitivity generally,
  with no prediction tied to training-data provenance.

## How it could be wrong

- The gap may be dominated by ordinary shortcut learning, swamping any contamination signal.
  Distinguishing them needs a control: perturbed items built from material provably outside
  the cutoff.
- "Trained on synthetic data" is not cleanly knowable for most open models — the independent
  variable is often unavailable, which limits how strong a comparison can be built.
- Small models may simply be bad at perturbed items for capability reasons, with no
  contamination involved. Needs a matched-capability control, which is hard to construct
  honestly.

## Route to a claim

Direct extension of `projects/001-geometric-reasoning` E001: the same harness, grouping
models by training-data provenance instead of by size. No extra infrastructure — one extra
column in the run record, and a preregistered grouping decided before the numbers exist.
