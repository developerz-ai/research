## Proposition

One sentence, quantified wherever possible. "Smaller models can reason better" is not a
claim; "a 124M model trained with X reaches ≥0.6 flip rate on paired perturbations, where
an equally-trained baseline reaches ≤0.4" is.

## Falsifier

**Write this before any code exists.**

The concrete result that kills the claim. Not "if it doesn't work" — a number, a
comparison, a direction.

> This claim is dead if:

If you cannot fill that in, this is not a claim yet. Move it to open questions and say so.

## Operationalisation

The gap between the idea's vocabulary and something measurable is where most of the
intellectual work lives. Be explicit about every choice, because each is a place the test
could fail to be a test of the claim.

## Cheap test first

The smallest experiment that could plausibly kill it. Prefer this over the definitive one —
a claim killed in twenty minutes is worth more than a claim confirmed in a week, and most
claims die cheaply if they are going to die at all.

## Reachability

Can this be decided on the reference hardware (RTX 3090 / CPU fallback)? If not, say so
plainly and either reformulate to something reachable or mark it out of scope. Do not
quietly test something easier and let the writeup imply it settled the original question.
